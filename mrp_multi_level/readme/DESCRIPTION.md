This module adds a multi-level MRP scheduler to Odoo. Based on current
stock, confirmed demand and expected supply, it computes net
requirements for every product and proposes replenishment through
**Planned Orders**.

A Planned Order is a replenishment proposal, not a real document: it
states *what* to order, *how much*, *when* it is due and *when* it
should be released. Nothing is purchased, manufactured or transferred
until a planner reviews the proposals and releases them, which creates
the corresponding draft purchase orders, manufacturing orders or
transfers. This introduces a review step that Odoo's standard
scheduler does not have.

The calculation starts at the top level of the bills of material and
explodes demand down to the lowest level, so manufactured products
generate dependent demand on their components automatically.

## Concepts

The module is built around a few key concepts:

- **MRP Area**: a planning scope tied to a stock location within a
  warehouse. Each area is planned independently, which allows different
  replenishment policies per warehouse or sub-location. *Example: a
  company plans its main warehouse in the `WH/Stock` area and a
  satellite repair shop in a second area, so stock in the shop does not
  count as available for the main warehouse and vice versa.*
- **Product MRP Area Parameters**: the planning parameters of a product
  in a given area (safety stock, ordering constraints, lead times...).
  A product is only planned in the areas where it has parameters.
  *Example: the same screw can have a safety stock of 500 units in the
  main warehouse area and only 20 in the satellite area.*
- **MRP Move**: a single demand or supply element considered by the
  calculation — an open stock move, an unconfirmed purchase order,
  demand exploded from a BoM, or a forecast provided by extension
  modules. MRP moves are the inputs of the netting process and are
  recreated on every run. *Example: a confirmed delivery of 10 units
  next Friday creates a demand move of -10; a confirmed PO for 30 units
  creates a supply move of +30; a planned production of FP-2 explodes
  into demand moves on its components SF-1 and SF-2.*
- **Planned Order**: a replenishment proposal generated by the run,
  with a release date, a due date and a quantity. Planned orders are
  released into real purchase orders, manufacturing orders or
  transfers. *Fixed* orders are kept across runs; unfixed ones are
  recalculated. *Example: a shortage of 10 units of FP-2 due on Friday
  with a 1-day lead time produces a planned order with due date Friday
  and release date Thursday, with action "Manufacturing Order".*
- **MRP Inventory**: the time-phased result of the run — one line per
  product and day showing demand, supply and the projected stock level.
  *Example: with 20 units on hand, a demand of 15 on Tuesday and a
  planned supply of 50 on Wednesday, the projection shows a forecasted
  inventory of 5 on Tuesday and 55 on Wednesday.*
- **Low Level Code (LLC)**: the deepest level at which a product
  appears in any bill of material. The calculation processes products
  by increasing LLC so that dependent demand is always propagated
  top-down. *Example: in the structure FP-2 → SF-1 → PP-1, FP-2 has LLC
  0, SF-1 has LLC 1 and PP-1 has LLC 2 — even though PP-1 is also a
  direct component of FP-1, its deepest level wins.*
- **Supply Method**: how a product is replenished — *Buy*, *Produce*,
  *Kit*, *Pull From*, *Push To* or *Pull & Push* — derived from its
  routes and bills of material. *Example: FP-2 has the Manufacture
  route and a BoM, so its supply method is Produce and shortages
  propose manufacturing orders; PP-1 has the Buy route and proposes
  purchase orders; SF-3 uses a kit (phantom) BoM, so its demand passes
  straight through to its components.*

## Key Features

- MRP parameters defined per product variant and MRP area: safety
  stock, minimum/maximum/multiple order quantities, demand grouping,
  lead times and more.
- Daily cron job and manual wizard to run the MRP calculation, either
  for all MRP areas or a selected subset.
- Planned Orders with release and due dates; manually created or
  "fixed" orders are preserved across runs.
- Time-phased MRP Inventory view showing demand, supply, forecasted
  stock and quantity to procure per day.
- Procurement wizard to release planned orders: creates draft purchase
  orders, manufacturing orders or internal transfers depending on the
  product routes.
- Multi-company and multi-warehouse aware through MRP Areas.
- Extensible: hooks for custom forecast sources and additional
  demand/supply inputs.

## Key extensions

The MRP calculation is designed to be extended. These OCA modules build
on top of it:

- `mrp_multi_level_estimate`: integrates the *Stock
  Demand Estimates* system (`stock_demand_estimate`) so that statistical
  demand forecasts enter the MRP run as extra demand moves. It installs
  automatically when both modules are present, and adds a *Group Days of
  Estimates* setting to control how estimates are consolidated.
